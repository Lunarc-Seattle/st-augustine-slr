St. Augustine, Florida · NOAA SLR scenarios above MHHW

St. Augustine under rising water

At just 2 ft of sea-level rise, low-lying ground at the Castillo de San Marcos is already under water. At 3 ft, a fifth of the city's land floods beyond today's high-tide line, and the sea reaches the Plaza de la Constitución.

A reproducible pipeline built from NOAA, U.S. Census and OpenStreetMap data. Source code: github.com/Lunarc-Seattle/st-augustine-slr

    Method

    • City boundary from TIGER/Line 2025, projected to NAD83 / UTM 17N so every area is in m².
    • City land is the boundary minus Census water, so open water never counts as land lost.
    • NOAA slr and low layers are clipped, dissolved and intersected with land at each level.
    • Land already inundated at 0 ft is the baseline; the headline numbers are new inundation only.
    • Every run checks itself: 0 ft new loss is zero, slr never shrinks with level, and slr and low never overlap.

    Limits

    • NOAA's layers are a bathtub model on a lidar DEM: no storm surge, waves, erosion or future defences.
    • Whether low areas actually flood depends on drainage and groundwater.
    • The OSM extract is frozen at its query time; site counts are a lower bound.
